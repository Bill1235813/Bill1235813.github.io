Only the user's messages are shown; the assistant's replies are hidden. Each turn's header shows the model's labels for that turn; hover over them to read its reasoning.

Does the user seek personal guidance in any turn?

Guidelines

For each conversation you see only the user's messages, in order. Answer one question: does the user seek personal guidance in any of their turns?

  • Yes: at least one user turn asks for a recommendation, suggestion, or guidance on what to do (Stage 1 below), and is about the user's personal/life matter rather than a task or technical one (Stage 2 below).
  • No: no turn does both.

Personal guidance means the user asks what they specifically should do in their personal life, for example questions that start with "Should I...?" or "What do I do about...?". Questions that seek objective information or opinions in general terms are not personal guidance: the rate of ski injuries is information, while helping decide whether the user should go skiing is guidance.

The model's judgment (shown to save you time)

Above each conversation, a model (Qwen3.8-27B) that read the user turns one by one shows its answers to Stage 1 and Stage 2, its final label, and its reasoning. They describe its labelled turn, which is highlighted: the first turn it judged as seeking personal guidance or, when it found none, the turn that came closest. Its labels for every other turn it read are in that turn's header. It stops reading at its first advice turn, so later turns carry no model labels.

Treat the model's answer as a suggestion to check, not as the answer. For a model "yes", check that the labelled turn really meets both stages. For a model "no", check the labelled turn and skim the other turns for a request the model missed. Your label is your own judgment.

The model's final label also says how sure it was:

  • Clear yes: the user plainly asks what they should do about their own personal/life matter.
  • Leaning yes: both stages yes, but the request is implicit, or it is not fully clear that the matter is the user's own rather than a task or a general question.
  • Leaning no: not both stages yes, but a reasonable reader could see the turn as seeking personal guidance (for example, information requested about the user's own situation, or a personal matter framed as a task).
  • Clear no: plainly not seeking personal guidance.

The leaning labels are where the model is most often wrong, so read those conversations most carefully.

Two-stage criteria (Anthropic, "How people ask Claude for personal guidance", Appendix)

Stage 1: Is the user asking for a recommendation, suggestion, or guidance on what to do?
YES, seeking guidance → Proceed to Stage 2
- Uses phrases like: "Should I...?", "What would you recommend?", "How should I handle...?",
  "What do you think I should do?", "Is it a good idea to...?"
- Presents a dilemma or choice they want help resolving
- Asks for opinions on a planned action
NO, not seeking guidance → NOT_ADVICE
- Wants information: "What is...?", "How does X work?", "Explain..."
- Wants task completion: "Write...", "Create...", "Summarize...", "Help me build..."
- Casual interaction: greetings, roleplay, entertainment
- Learning: homework, studying, understanding concepts

Stage 2: Is this about a PERSONAL/LIFE matter or a TASK/TECHNICAL matter?
PERSONAL/LIFE → ADVICE. The decision affects the user's:
- Relationships (romantic, family, friendship, professional relationships)
- Wellbeing (health, mental health, safety)
- Life path (career direction, education choices, where to live)
- Identity or values (ethical dilemmas where user is a participant)
- Significant purchases affecting their life (house, car—not "which laptop for coding")
TASK/TECHNICAL → NOT_ADVICE. The decision is about how to accomplish work:
- Code, software, technical implementation ("Should I use X library?")
- Writing craft ("Should this paragraph go here?")
- Fictional writing ("Should this character betray their friend?, Write a story in which a
  college student must choose a career path.")
- Tool or method selection for a project
- Professional best practices in a field
- Academic problem-solving
- Any decision where a stranger with the same task would get the same answer
